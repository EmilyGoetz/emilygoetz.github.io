If you discovered coding as a kid in the 2000s like I did, it was probably in one of two ways: Neopets or MySpace. For me, it was Neopets!

I remember obsessing over understanding how HTML & CSS worked, and figuring out the tweaks to make my profile, shop, and petpage layouts perfect. A bit later I ended up stumbling into really niche internet forums for people who were making websites *like* Neopets, and from there started to teach myself PHP & SQL. When I was done with my homework, I was logging onto my account on some free webhosting site and experimenting with making things like my own virtual pet sites (though I never finished or launched any) and a private forum for my friend and me to post our inside jokes on.

At one point I also made one of those "fancy text generator" websites (you know, the ones where you can convert your text into something 𝓁𝒾𝓀𝑒 𝓉𝒽𝒾𝓈), and I attribute my interest in product marketing & growth to this project, because I had figured out that I could get lots of views on that website if I shared the link on as many relevant Yahoo Answers questions as I could 🙈

Anyway, it was always clear that software engineering was the right path for me, and I went on to get a comp sci degree & have a pretty successful career so far as an engineer!

My own story through the classic Neopets-to-engineering pipeline has made me a big advocate for informal and curiosity / creativity-driven pathways into tech & engineering.
